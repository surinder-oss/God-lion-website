How Much Does It Cost to Discharge a Mortgage in Alberta?

What It Means to Discharge a Mortgage

When you get a mortgage in Alberta, your lender registers it on your property's title at Land Titles. That registration is the lender's claim on your home. It's what lets them act if the loan isn't paid.

Paying the loan to zero does not remove that claim on its own. A mortgage discharge is the formal step that takes the lender off your title. The lender signs a discharge document, it gets registered at Land Titles, and your title is updated.

Until that happens, anyone who searches your title will still see the old mortgage. That matters when you sell, refinance or borrow against your home later.

When You Need to Discharge a Mortgage

Most people discharge a mortgage in one of these situations:

  • You sell your home. The buyer needs a clean title, so your mortgage is paid out from the sale money and then discharged.
  • You switch to a new lender. At renewal or mid-term, the old lender's mortgage comes off and the new lender's goes on.
  • You refinance with a different lender. Same idea. The old charge is removed so the new one can be registered.
  • You pay it off completely. This is the one people forget. Even after the final payment, the mortgage stays on title until it's discharged.

If you renew with your current lender, there's usually nothing to discharge. The same mortgage stays on title and only the terms change.

What It Costs to Discharge a Mortgage in Alberta

Here are the usual costs to discharge a mortgage in Alberta, as of 2026:

  • Lender discharge fee: usually somewhere between nothing and about $400. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada gives this same range and notes that banks must list the fee in your mortgage contract.
  • Payout statement fee: some lenders charge a small fee to prepare the statement that shows exactly what you owe. Many don't.
  • Legal fees: if a lawyer handles the discharge, expect a fee for that work. When you're selling or switching, it's usually part of the lawyer's overall bill for the deal rather than a separate charge.
  • Land Titles registration: small. Alberta's Land Titles fee schedule lists discharge documents at $25 to $35 as of October 2026.
  • Prepayment penalty: only if you pay off the mortgage before your term ends. This can range from a few thousand dollars to much more. More on this below.

Here's a simple example. Say you sell your home right at the end of your term, so there's no penalty. Your lender charges a $300 discharge fee, Land Titles charges $25, and your lawyer handles it as part of the sale. Your discharge costs come to about $325 plus your share of the legal bill.

Now say you sell two years early on a fixed rate. The same fees apply, but now there's also a penalty. On a large balance, that penalty can easily be the biggest number on your payout statement.

If you're refinancing with a new lender rather than selling, the discharge is only one piece of the bill. Our breakdown of the cost to refinance a mortgage in Alberta covers the appraisal, legal and registration costs too.

The Big One: Breaking Your Term Early

If you discharge a mortgage before the end of its term, you're ending a contract early. Your lender will charge a prepayment penalty.

  • Variable rate: usually three months' interest.
  • Fixed rate: usually the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD). The IRD is roughly the interest the lender loses because you left early, and it's the one that surprises people.

Every lender works out the IRD a bit differently, so two people with the same balance can get very different numbers. Our guide on how mortgage penalties are calculated in Canada walks through the math with examples.

The good news is there are often ways to shrink or avoid the penalty:

  • Time it with your maturity date. At the end of your term, there's no penalty at all. If you're selling within a few months of renewal, this alone can save thousands.
  • Use your prepayment room first. Many closed mortgages let you pay down a set amount each year without a penalty. Using it before you pay out can lower the balance the penalty is based on.
  • Port the mortgage. If you're selling and buying, you may be able to take your mortgage to the new home. See our guide on porting a mortgage in Canada.
  • Ask for the number in writing. Get an exact penalty quote from your lender before you sign anything. Estimates from online calculators are only a starting point.

How the Mortgage Discharge Process Works in Alberta

In most cases, a lawyer handles the discharge for you. Here's what happens behind the scenes:

  1. Payout statement. Your lawyer (or you) asks the lender for a payout statement. It shows your balance, interest to the payout date, the discharge fee and any penalty.
  2. Payment. The mortgage is paid in full. On a sale, your lawyer pays it from the sale money. On a switch, the new lender's funds pay it out.
  3. Discharge document. Once the lender gets its money, it signs the discharge and sends it on.
  4. Registration. The discharge is filed at Alberta Land Titles, and the mortgage comes off your title.

This last step can take a few weeks after closing, because it depends on the lender sending the paperwork. On a sale, the buyer's lawyer usually accepts your lawyer's promise to get the discharge registered, so the sale can close on time.

If you're moving to a new lender at renewal, a lawyer or the new lender's legal team usually takes care of the discharge as part of the switch. Our post on switching lenders at renewal covers that path in more detail.

Paid Off Your Mortgage? Check Your Title

This is where people get caught. You make your last payment, the lender sends a nice letter, and you assume it's done. Years later, you go to sell or borrow, and the old mortgage is still on title.

Some lenders register the discharge for you after the final payment. Others only send it if you ask. The FCAC notes that most lenders send confirmation only on request. Call your lender, ask how they handle discharges, and get it in writing.

Two more things to watch:

  • Collateral mortgages. Some mortgages are registered for more than you borrowed, so you can borrow again later without new paperwork. That registration can stay on title even at a zero balance until you ask for it to be removed.
  • Home equity lines of credit. If you have a HELOC tied to the same registration, it usually needs to be paid off and closed before the lender will discharge. Leaving it open with a zero balance keeps the lender on title.

Sometimes keeping the registration makes sense. If you want a line of credit available for later, removing the charge means paying to register a new one when you need it. It's worth a short conversation before you decide.

Does the Type of Lender Change the Cost?

The basic process is the same at every lender, but the fees and penalties can differ.

  • Banks and credit unions (A-lenders): the discharge fee is set out in your contract. Fixed-rate penalties at some of these lenders can be large because of how they work out the IRD.
  • Alternative lenders (B-lenders): terms are often shorter, and the fees and penalty rules are set out in your commitment. Some include an admin fee to discharge.
  • Private lenders: terms are usually short, often one or two years. The contract may set a fixed number of months' interest as a penalty, or allow payout without one after a set time, plus a discharge or admin fee.

Your mortgage documents are the final word. If you can't find the numbers, your lender has to give them to you, and a broker can help you read them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to discharge a mortgage in Alberta?

Most people pay a lender discharge fee of nothing up to about $400, a small Land Titles fee, and legal fees if a lawyer handles it. If you pay off the mortgage before your term ends, add a prepayment penalty, which is often the largest cost. Check your mortgage contract for your lender's exact fee.

Who pays the mortgage discharge fee when you sell a house?

The seller pays it. The fee, the balance owing and any penalty are taken from the sale money before the seller gets the rest. Your lawyer handles the payout and makes sure the discharge is registered after closing.

Is a mortgage automatically discharged when it's paid off?

No. Paying the balance to zero doesn't remove the mortgage from your title. The lender has to sign a discharge and it has to be registered at Land Titles. Some lenders do this on their own, but many only do it when you ask, so confirm with yours.

How long does it take to discharge a mortgage in Alberta?

It often takes a few weeks after the mortgage is paid, because the lender has to prepare and send the discharge before it's registered. On a sale, closing doesn't have to wait for it. The lawyers handle it with a written promise that the discharge will follow.

Can I avoid a penalty when I discharge my mortgage?

Often, yes. There's no penalty at the end of your term, and you may be able to use your yearly prepayment room or port the mortgage to a new home. Ask your lender for a written penalty quote first, then compare your options.
