Can You Get a Joint Mortgage When One Partner Has Bad Credit?

One of you has a credit score in the high 700s. The other is sitting closer to 580 after a rough couple of years. You want to buy a home together, or your renewal is coming up, and you're worried the lower score will pull you both down.

It can matter. But a joint mortgage with bad credit on one side is not a dead end. How much it matters depends on how the lender reads two scores, whose income you need to qualify, and which kind of lender you go to. Here's how it really works, in plain words.

How Lenders Read Two Credit Scores on One Application

When two people apply together, the lender pulls a credit report for each of you. Canada has two credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion. Your score can be a little different at each one, because not every company reports to both.

Here's the part most couples get wrong. Lenders don't simply average your scores. They don't add 810 and 600 and call it 705. Each lender has its own method:

  • Some treat the main income earner's score as the lead score for the file.
  • Some look at the lowest score on the application.
  • Almost all of them read both full reports, not just the two numbers.

For insured mortgages (less than 20% down), there's one rule that helps couples. CMHC's purchase guidelines say at least one borrower or guarantor must have a credit score of 600 or more. So the insurer's minimum doesn't have to be met by both of you. Keep in mind that lenders can set their own higher minimums on top of that, and many do.

The story behind the number matters too. A 620 caused by high card balances reads very differently from a 620 with a recent collection or an open consumer proposal. The first can often be fixed in a month or two. The second usually needs time or a different type of lender.

What One Lower Score Can Change

A weaker score on one side doesn't cancel out a strong one. But it can shift a few things:

  • Which lenders will look at the file. If the lower score is well under what a bank wants, the file may need an alternative (B) lender instead.
  • The rate and fees. Alternative lenders charge more than banks, and there's often a lender fee. Rates depend on the full file.
  • How much debt counts against you. Every borrower's debts go into the debt ratios. If the partner with lower credit also has a big car loan or maxed-out cards, the payments can hurt more than the score itself.

One thing doesn't change. On a joint mortgage, you're both responsible for the whole balance, not half each. A missed payment shows up on both credit reports. Before you sign together, make sure you both understand that.

Your Options for a Joint Mortgage With Bad Credit

There isn't one right answer. The best path depends on your income, your down payment, and what's behind the lower score. Here are the main choices, from the lowest cost to the highest.

1. Apply together with a bank or credit union

If the stronger borrower earns most of the income, and the lower score isn't too far off or has a clear reason behind it, many banks and credit unions will still approve the file. On an insured mortgage, one borrower at 600 or higher meets the insurer's floor. The lender's own rules still have to fit.

2. Apply with only the stronger partner

If one income can qualify on its own, you can leave the other person off the mortgage. Then the lender only looks at one credit report and one set of debts. The trade-off is a smaller approval, since you lose the second income.

Title is a separate question. Many lenders want everyone on title to be on the mortgage too, so ask before you plan around it. And in Alberta, if you're married and the home is in one spouse's name, the other spouse is usually asked to sign a Dower Act consent at the lawyer's office.

3. Add a guarantor

A guarantor backs the mortgage without owning the home. Their credit and income can strengthen a thin or bruised file. Rules here vary a lot. Some lenders only accept a spouse as a guarantor, and some products don't allow guarantors at all. Our guide to parents helping adult children buy explains the difference between a co-signer and a guarantor.

4. Go to an alternative (B) lender

B-lenders look at the whole picture instead of a strict checklist. Depending on the overall file, they can approve scores below 600, and they can allow debt ratios up to roughly 50% GDS and TDS, compared with the usual 39% and 44% at a bank. Many couples use a one- to three-year term, rebuild the weaker score, then move back to a bank at renewal. Our B-lender mortgage guide walks through how that works.

5. Use a private lender as a short bridge

Private lenders focus on the property and your equity more than your credit. Expect to need a bigger down payment, usually 20% to 25% or more, along with a short term and higher costs. It's a bridge, not a long-term plan.

6. Wait a few months and rebuild

Sometimes the smartest move is time. Six to twelve months of on-time payments and lower card balances can lift a score enough to change your options. Our article on the minimum credit score for a mortgage in Canada shows what each score range usually opens up.

Renewing or Refinancing a Joint Mortgage With Bad Credit

A lot of couples hit this problem mid-term, not at purchase. One partner's credit drops after a job loss, a separation, or a business that struggled. Here's how that plays out.

  • Renewing with your current lender. Most lenders don't make you qualify again at renewal, so a lower score usually doesn't block it. Watch the offer closely, though. A weaker file can get a less competitive renewal offer, and some lenders do decline to renew.
  • Switching lenders at renewal. Since November 21, 2024, a straight switch with no extra money and no longer amortization doesn't need the stress test. The new lender still pulls both credit reports, though, so the lower score can limit who will take you. Our guide on switching lenders at renewal without the stress test covers the details.
  • Refinancing. A refinance is a brand-new application. Both reports get pulled and both of you must qualify again.
  • Taking one person off the mortgage. The person staying usually has to qualify alone, on their own income and credit. That's often done as a refinance or a spousal buyout. If the person staying is the one with the lower score, a B-lender or private option may be the bridge.

Simple Steps Before You Apply Together

  1. Both of you check your own reports. You can get them free from Equifax and TransUnion. Look for errors, old debts that should be closed, and accounts you don't recognize.
  2. Pay card balances down. High balances are the fastest thing to fix, and lowering them can move a score within a billing cycle or two.
  3. Write down the story. What happened, when it happened, and what's fixed now. Lenders give more room when the reason is clear and in the past.
  4. Don't open new credit. A new car loan or store card right before you apply can undo the work.
  5. Talk to a broker first. One review can show which lenders fit both files, instead of applying at bank after bank and collecting hard credit checks.
  6. Be honest about everything. If there's a proposal, a collection, or a support payment on either side, say so up front. Surprises hurt a file far more than the facts do.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a joint mortgage if one person has bad credit?

Often, yes. On an insured mortgage, CMHC only needs one borrower or guarantor with a score of 600 or more, though lenders can ask for more. If a bank won't approve the file, an alternative lender may, or the stronger partner can sometimes apply alone.

Do lenders use the lower credit score on a joint mortgage?

It depends on the lender. Some focus on the main income earner's score, some look at the lowest score, and most read both full reports. They don't simply average the two numbers.

Can I leave my partner off the mortgage because of their credit?

Yes, if your income qualifies on its own. The lender then only looks at your credit and debts, but your approval amount may be smaller. Ask about title before you decide, since many lenders want everyone on title on the mortgage too.

What credit score do you need for a joint mortgage in Canada?

For an insured mortgage, at least one borrower or guarantor needs 600 or higher under CMHC rules, and many banks want more. Alternative lenders can go below 600 depending on the overall file. Private lenders focus mostly on equity.

Does a joint mortgage affect both credit scores?

Yes. The mortgage shows up on both credit reports, and you're both responsible for the full balance. On-time payments help both scores, and a missed payment hurts both.
