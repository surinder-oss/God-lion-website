Getting a Mortgage After Credit Counselling: How a Debt Management Plan Affects You

You did the hard part. You called a credit counsellor, signed up for a debt management plan, and started paying down what you owe. Now you want to buy a home, and you're wondering if that plan is going to stand in the way.

Here's the honest answer. Getting a mortgage after credit counselling is possible. It takes some time, a clean record after the plan, and the right lender for where you are today. This guide explains how lenders read a debt management plan, what they want to see, and how long it usually takes.

What a Debt Management Plan Actually Is

A debt management plan is set up through a credit counselling agency, usually a non-profit. The counsellor works out a deal with your creditors so you make one monthly payment to the agency. The agency then pays each creditor for you.

The counsellor will usually ask your creditors to lower or drop the interest and fees. In return, you pay back the full amount you owe. Plans are usually built to finish within about five years.

A debt management plan is not the same as a consumer proposal. A consumer proposal is a legal process run by a Licensed Insolvency Trustee, and it usually settles your debt for less than you owe. A debt management plan is an informal deal, and creditors can say no to it. The Government of Canada's debt help page explains both options in plain terms.

One thing many people don't know: just talking to a credit counsellor does not hurt your credit score. It's signing up for a plan that shows up on your report.

How Credit Counselling Shows Up on Your Credit Report

While you're in a debt management plan, the accounts in the plan usually show an R7 rating. R7 means you're making regular payments through a special arrangement. Your cards in the plan are normally closed, too.

After you finish the plan, the notation usually comes off your report about two to three years later. The exact timing depends on the credit bureau, and sources don't all agree. So pull both your Equifax and TransUnion reports and check the dates yourself. Both are free to request.

Here's what matters for a mortgage after credit counselling. Many lenders put credit counselling in the same group as collections, consumer proposals and bankruptcy. They see it as a major credit event, even though you're paying back every dollar. That doesn't mean a "no." It means the lender will look harder at what happened after the plan.

The good news: your score can start to recover while you're still in the plan, as long as every payment is on time.

Can You Get a Mortgage While Still in a Debt Management Plan?

Sometimes, yes. But your options are narrower while the plan is open. Different lenders treat it in different ways.

  • Banks and credit unions (A-lenders): most want the plan finished and some new credit built on top of it. With less than 20% down, your mortgage needs default insurance, and the insurers also look for re-established credit after the plan. At least one borrower usually needs a credit score of 600 or higher for an insured mortgage.
  • Alternative lenders (B-lenders): some will look at a file during or just after a plan. They often want a bigger down payment, commonly 20% or more. Depending on the full file, some can work with scores below 600 and debt ratios up to roughly 50% / 50% (GDS/TDS). Terms are usually one to three years, with a higher rate and often a lender fee.
  • Private lenders: these focus mostly on your equity and down payment, usually 20% to 25% or more. Terms are short and fees are higher. Private money works best as a bridge to a better mortgage, not a long-term home.

Your monthly budget can actually look better during a plan, because your debt payments are often lower. But every lender sees the R7 on your report, so the plan itself is the bigger question, not just the payment.

What Lenders Want to See After Your Plan Ends

Once the plan is done, you can build a much stronger file. Here's what most lenders will ask about.

  1. Your completion letter. Ask the agency for a letter that shows the plan is paid in full. Keep a copy with your other documents.
  2. New credit, used well. Lenders want proof you can handle credit again. A common target is two credit accounts, like a secured credit card and a small loan or a second card, paid on time. Many A-lenders like to see one to two years of clean history after the plan.
  3. Your own savings. Have your down payment and closing costs in your account, with about three months of bank statements to show where the money came from. Some lenders limit gifted down payments for borrowers with past credit events.
  4. A short explanation letter. One page is enough. Say what caused the debt, such as a job loss, separation or illness, and what you changed after.
  5. Steady income. Same job or same line of work, with pay stubs and tax papers that match what you put on the application.

A Realistic Timeline for a Mortgage After Credit Counselling

Every file is different, but this is a common path.

  • While you're in the plan: make every payment on time. Save what you can. Don't open new credit unless your counsellor says it's okay, since it can break the terms of your plan.
  • In the last year of the plan: pull your credit reports and talk to a broker. You want a clear plan for the day the program ends.
  • Right after the plan ends: if you have a large down payment, a B-lender may be an option now. You'd usually plan to move to a regular lender once your credit has been rebuilt.
  • One to two years after the plan: with new credit paid on time and savings in place, many banks and credit unions may consider your file, including insured mortgages if you meet the score and debt ratio rules.

For example, picture someone who finished their plan last spring. They opened a secured card the same month and have paid it on time ever since. With 20% saved, a B-lender may look at them today. If they wait another year and add a second credit account, the bank door may open, often at a lower cost.

Credit Counselling vs a Consumer Proposal: Which Is Easier for a Mortgage?

People often ask which one looks better to a lender. There isn't one answer for everyone.

With a debt management plan, you repay the full amount, and some lenders give you credit for that. A consumer proposal is a legal settlement for less than you owe, and it stays on your report for three years after you finish or six years from the date you filed, whichever comes first. Both usually show as R7, and both usually need rebuilt credit before the bank will say yes.

The right choice for your debt is a talk to have with a credit counsellor or a Licensed Insolvency Trustee. If you're already in a proposal, our guide to getting a mortgage after a consumer proposal covers that path step by step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a mortgage after credit counselling?

Yes, many people do. Most banks want the plan finished and one to two years of new credit paid on time. Alternative and private lenders may consider you sooner, usually with a larger down payment.

How long does a debt management plan stay on your credit report?

The R7 notation usually comes off about two to three years after you finish the plan, depending on the credit bureau. Pull your Equifax and TransUnion reports to see the exact dates on your file.

Can I get a mortgage while on a debt management plan?

It's possible, but harder. Most banks will wait until the plan is done. Some alternative and private lenders may approve a mortgage during a plan if you have a good down payment, often 20% or more, and steady income.

Does credit counselling hurt your credit score?

Talking to a credit counsellor does not affect your score. Joining a debt management plan does, because the accounts in the plan are reported as R7. Your score can recover over time with on-time payments.

How much down payment do I need after a debt management plan?

Once your credit is rebuilt, you may qualify with the regular minimum of 5% on the first $500,000. Right after a plan, alternative lenders usually want 20% or more, and private lenders usually want 20% to 25% or more.
