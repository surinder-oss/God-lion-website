What Buying a Home in Medicine Hat Costs in 2026

Here's the local picture from the Medicine Hat Real Estate Board's August 2026 report for the city:

  • All homes: 98 sales, an average price of $378,372, up 0.6% from a year ago.
  • Detached: 77 of those 98 sales, at an average of $413,145, down 0.9% from a year ago.
  • Row homes: an average of $262,267, up 3%.
  • Apartments: an average of $220,991, up 8%.
  • Supply: 191 homes listed and 1.95 months of supply.

That last number matters most. Under about two months of supply is a seller's market. Calgary was closer to four months in August. So even though prices in Medicine Hat are lower, good homes can still move fast. Inventory is up about 32% from last year, so buyers have a bit more room than they did. But you still want your financing ready before you write an offer.

One note on the numbers. Medicine Hat reports an average price, and Calgary reports a benchmark price. They're measured differently, so treat the comparison as a rough guide, not an exact one. You can read the full monthly reports on the Medicine Hat Real Estate Board stats page.

The Down Payment Math in Medicine Hat

This is where buying a home in Medicine Hat really helps first-time buyers. For a home you'll live in, the minimum down payment is 5% of the first $500,000 and 10% of any part above that.

Take a detached home at the August average of about $413,000. The minimum down payment is around $20,700. On a Calgary detached home near $744,000, the minimum is about $49,400. That's more than twice as much to save for a similar type of home.

With less than 20% down, the mortgage needs default insurance. The premium gets added to your mortgage, not paid up front. Lenders will also want to see that you have about 1.5% of the price set aside for closing costs. On a $413,000 home, that's roughly $6,200.

Alberta has no land transfer tax, which helps too. You pay land titles registration fees instead, on the transfer and again on the mortgage. On a home in this price range, those two fees together usually come to under $1,000. Our guide to closing costs in Alberta walks through the full list, line by line.

Check the Flood Map Before You Firm Up

The South Saskatchewan River runs right through Medicine Hat, and so do Seven Persons Creek and Ross Creek. The 2013 flood hit the city hard. Since then, the City has built berms and dikes, stabilized riverbanks, and added sewer backup protection. But the City itself says some low-lying areas have a history of overland flooding.

Here's why that matters for your mortgage. Every lender needs proof of home insurance before your mortgage funds. Basic coverage is usually easy. Overland flood coverage is a separate add-on, and in higher-risk spots it can cost a lot more or be hard to get. You don't want to find that out two days before closing.

So if you're buying a home in Medicine Hat near the river or a creek, do this before your conditions come off:

  • Look up the address on the province's flood hazard maps. Alberta splits flood areas into the floodway, where water runs deepest and fastest, and the flood fringe, where it's shallower and slower.
  • Get a real insurance quote on the exact home, with overland flood and sewer backup included. Ask what the deductible is.
  • Add the cost to your budget. A high insurance premium doesn't change your debt ratios much, but it does change what the home costs you each month.
  • Ask the seller about any past water in the basement, and ask your home inspector to look for it.

A home near the river isn't a deal-breaker. Plenty of people buy there with no trouble. The point is to know before you're locked in.

Older Homes, Utilities and Acreages Around the Hat

A lot of Medicine Hat's affordable homes are older bungalows and split-levels. Older homes can have wiring, plumbing or heating that some lenders and insurers ask about. Knob-and-tube wiring, old plumbing and buried oil tanks are the usual ones. None of these is automatically a no, but they can slow down an approval or an insurance quote. Our post on financing an older home in Canada covers what lenders look for and how to handle it.

Medicine Hat is also a bit different on utilities. The City owns and runs its own natural gas and electric utilities, which is unusual in Alberta. For your mortgage, the key point is simple. Heating is always part of your debt-service math. Many lenders use a standard heating figure, and some use your actual cost. Either way, it's in there.

If you're looking just outside the city, in Redcliff, Dunmore or Cypress County, you may run into acreages. A lender will look at the well, the septic, the size of the land and how much of the value sits in the house versus the land. We saw the same pattern when we wrote about buying a home in Red Deer and its nearby acreages. It pays to line up a lender that's comfortable with rural homes early.

How Medicine Hat Income Gets Underwritten

The Hat runs on natural gas, trades, agriculture, health care and small business. It also draws a lot of retirees. Each of those incomes gets read a little differently.

Salaried and hourly workers are the simplest. A letter of employment and a recent pay stub usually cover it, with overtime or shift pay averaged over time.

Self-employed, farm and contract income is where files get tricky. Here's the real range:

  • Banks and credit unions (A-lenders) usually average your last two years of declared income from your tax returns. Some have stated-income programs for business owners. Debt ratios usually land around 39/44, and you'll need to pass the stress test.
  • Alternative lenders (B-lenders) can often use about 12 months of business bank statements instead of your most recent tax return. They can stretch debt ratios toward roughly 50/50 and can work with a score below 600, depending on the whole file. Expect around 20% down and a lender fee.
  • Private lenders focus on your equity and down payment. They usually need 20% to 25% or more down, cost more, and work best with a clear plan to move to a regular lender.

Retirees often have strong savings but a smaller monthly income. Lenders can use pension income, CPP and OAS, usually confirmed with a T4A or bank statements showing the deposits. Some lenders gross up income that isn't taxed. And some programs look at your total assets as well as your monthly income. A single bank may not offer all of those options, which is why comparing matters.

Renewing in Medicine Hat? Don't Just Sign

Not buying? If your mortgage is coming up for renewal, the letter from your lender is a starting point, not a final offer. Since late 2024, you can switch to a new lender at renewal without re-qualifying at the stress test, as long as you keep the same amount and amortization. That opens up more options, even if your income has changed since you bought.

Start looking about four months before your renewal date. That gives you time to compare offers and hold a rate if you want to. Our guide to switching lenders at renewal with no stress test explains how it works.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do you need to buy a house in Medicine Hat?

For a home you'll live in, the minimum down payment is 5% of the first $500,000. At the August 2026 detached average of about $413,000, that's around $20,700. You'll also need about 1.5% of the price for closing costs, and enough income to qualify.

Is Medicine Hat a buyer's or seller's market?

As of August 2026, Medicine Hat had about 1.95 months of supply, which leans toward a seller's market. Inventory was up from a year earlier, so buyers have a little more choice. Good homes can still sell quickly.

Can I get a mortgage on a home in a Medicine Hat flood zone?

Often, yes. The bigger question is insurance. Lenders need proof of home insurance before funding, and overland flood coverage can cost more or be harder to get in higher-risk areas. Check the provincial flood map and get a quote before you remove your conditions.

Can I get a mortgage in Medicine Hat if I'm self-employed or farm?

Yes, there are several paths. Banks usually average two years of declared income, while alternative lenders can often use about 12 months of business bank statements. Private lenders focus on equity. The right fit depends on your whole file.

Can retirees get a mortgage in Medicine Hat?

Yes. Lenders can use pension, CPP and OAS income, and some offer programs that look at your savings and investments too. Age alone isn't a reason to be declined. What matters is that the payments make sense for your income and assets.
