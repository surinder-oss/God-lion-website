Can You Renew Your Mortgage With Bad Credit? Here Is What Really Happens

Book a Free Consultation

You bought your home a few years ago with good credit. Since then, life happened. A job loss, a divorce, a medical leave, or a few months where the credit cards got away from you. Now your renewal letter is on its way, and you're wondering if your lender will even renew your mortgage.

Here's the short answer: yes, you can often renew a mortgage with bad credit, especially if you stay with your current lender and your mortgage payments have been on time. Where bad credit really bites is when you want to switch lenders, refinance, or when your mortgage payments themselves have fallen behind. Here's how each case works, in plain words.

Does Your Credit Score Affect Your Mortgage Renewal?

When you renew with the lender you already have, you usually don't have to qualify all over again. You already have the mortgage. The lender is simply offering you a new rate and a new term on the balance that's left.

That's why many lenders don't run a full credit check on a straight renewal when your payments have been on time. Some lenders do look at your credit report at renewal, often when something on the file worries them. Policies vary from lender to lender, so don't assume either way. Read the renewal letter, and if you're not sure, ask your lender directly if they'll be reviewing your credit.

A few things matter far more to your current lender than your score alone:

  • Your mortgage payment history. This is the big one. A lender sees every payment you've made to them.
  • Property tax arrears. Unpaid property taxes can rank ahead of the mortgage, so lenders take them seriously.
  • Collections or judgments against the property. Anything registered on your title gets noticed.
  • Changes you ask for. If you want more money, a longer amortization, or to add or remove a person, it stops being a simple renewal.

Federally regulated lenders, like the banks, must send you a renewal statement at least 21 days before your term ends, according to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada. They also have to tell you at least 21 days ahead if they won't renew. Credit unions follow provincial rules, which can differ.

Why Switching Lenders With Bad Credit Is Different

Switching lenders at renewal is a new application. The new lender will pull your credit, look at your income, and check your debts. If your score has dropped a lot since you bought, this is where it shows up.

There is some good news here. Since November 21, 2024, a straight switch at renewal doesn't have to pass the mortgage stress test. That applies when you keep the same balance and the same amortization. We explain how that works in our post on switching lenders at renewal with no stress test.

But the stress test exemption doesn't remove the credit check. A new bank or credit union still wants to see a credit history it's comfortable with. So if your credit took a hit, you may have fewer places to switch to, and the lender you have may be your strongest option for this term. That's not always bad. It just means you should know your options before you sign or shop.

One more tip: if you do shop around, try to keep all your applications within a short window, about two weeks. Credit bureaus tend to treat several mortgage inquiries close together as one shopping trip rather than many separate hits.

Missed Payments vs a Low Credit Score: Which One Matters?

People often lump these together, but lenders don't. There's a big difference between these two homeowners:

  • Homeowner A has a credit score of 590 because of a maxed-out credit card and a late car payment last year. Every mortgage payment has been on time.
  • Homeowner B has a score of 680, but missed two mortgage payments in the last six months.

At renewal, Homeowner A is often in the better position with their current lender. The lender's own experience with them is clean. Homeowner B worries the lender more, even with the higher score, because the missed payments are on the lender's own loan.

Late payments usually stay on your Equifax and TransUnion credit reports for about six years. Your score can recover well before that if your recent history is clean, which is why the last 12 to 24 months matter most to most lenders.

If your mortgage payments are behind right now, call your lender before the renewal date, not after. Lenders have more room to work with you when you reach out early. If you're in a consumer proposal, the rules are a bit different. Our post on mortgage renewal during a consumer proposal covers that situation step by step.

Your Options to Renew a Mortgage With Bad Credit

There isn't one path. The right one depends on your payment history, your equity, your income, and what went wrong with your credit. Here's the full range:

  1. Renew with your current lender and rebuild. If your payments are on time and the offer is fair, this is often the simplest move. Consider a shorter term, so you can shop again sooner once your credit has recovered.
  2. Negotiate the renewal offer. The first rate in the letter is not always the only one. Even with bruised credit, it's worth asking, especially if you have a clean payment record with that lender.
  3. Switch to another bank or credit union. Possible if your score and recent history still fit their rules. Some lenders accept lower scores than others, and a broker knows which ones look at the whole file.
  4. Move to an alternative (B) lender. B-lenders can work with credit scores below 600, depending on the overall file, and can allow debt ratios up to roughly 50% GDS and TDS, compared with the 39% and 44% most banks use. Rates and fees are higher, so this is usually a one- or two-term plan while you rebuild.
  5. Use a private lender as a short-term bridge. Private lenders focus mostly on your equity, often wanting you to keep at least 20% to 25% or more of the home's value. Costs are higher and terms are short, often one year. It can buy time when nobody else will say yes.

If your lender has already told you it won't renew, the clock matters. Our guide on what to do when your bank won't renew your mortgage walks through the first 21 days.

How to Rebuild Your Credit Before Your Next Renewal

The best time to fix your renewal is the term before it. If you take a shorter term now, use that time well:

  • Get your free credit reports from both Equifax and TransUnion and check them for mistakes. Errors are more common than people think, and you can dispute them.
  • Pay every bill on time. Set up automatic payments for at least the minimum on every card and loan.
  • Bring your card balances down. Try to keep each card under about 30% of its limit. Paying down revolving debt can move your score faster than almost anything else.
  • Don't close your oldest accounts. A longer credit history helps you.
  • Avoid new credit you don't need, like store cards or a new car loan, in the year before your renewal.
  • Start early. Many lenders let you lock in a rate up to about 120 days before your renewal date. Start looking at your options four to six months ahead, so you're never stuck signing whatever arrives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you renew a mortgage with bad credit?

Often, yes. If you stay with your current lender and your mortgage payments are up to date, many lenders will offer a renewal without asking you to qualify again. Switching to a new lender is harder, because that's a new application with a full credit check.

Do lenders check your credit when you renew your mortgage?

It depends on the lender. Many don't run a full credit check on a straight renewal when payments have been on time, but some do review your credit, especially if there are concerns. A new lender will always check your credit if you switch.

Will my bank renew my mortgage if my credit is bad?

In many cases, yes, because your payment history with the bank matters more than your score at renewal. A bank can still decide not to renew, and it must tell you at least 21 days before your term ends. Missed mortgage payments or property tax arrears raise that risk the most.

What happens if I can't renew my mortgage with my bank?

You can look at another bank or credit union, an alternative (B) lender, or a private lender for a short term. B-lenders can work with scores below 600 depending on the file, and private lenders focus mostly on your equity. Start early so you have time to compare.

Should I take a shorter term if my credit is bad at renewal?

A shorter term can make sense if you expect your credit to recover soon. It lets you shop again in one to three years with a stronger file. The trade-off is that you'll face another renewal sooner, so talk through both options before you sign.
