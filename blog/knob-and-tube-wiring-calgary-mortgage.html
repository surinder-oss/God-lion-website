Buying a Calgary Home With Knob-and-Tube Wiring: Insurance, Mortgage and What to Do

You found an older Calgary home you love. The offer is accepted. Then the inspection report says "knob-and-tube wiring," and the insurance company says no. Without insurance, your lender will not release the mortgage money, and you have a few days left on your financing condition.

This happens a lot with pre-1950 homes, and it does not have to kill the deal. Knob-and-tube is a sequencing problem. The wiring decides the insurance. The insurance decides whether the lender funds. Sort out that order before your conditions come off, and most of these deals can still close.

Below is how to spot knob-and-tube, why insurers are so careful with it, what a rewire costs in Calgary, what your lender actually checks, and the three ways buyers and sellers get these homes closed.

What Knob-and-Tube Wiring Is

Knob-and-tube (often shortened to K&T) was the normal way to wire a house in North America from the late 1800s until about the 1940s. Two separate copper wires run through the house. White ceramic knobs hold them away from the wood framing, and ceramic tubes carry them through the joists. There is no ground wire.

If it was put in properly and nobody has touched it since, it can still work. The trouble comes from age and from later changes:

  • No ground wire. Modern circuits have a ground wire to carry a fault away safely. K&T does not.
  • Old insulation on the wires. The cloth and rubber coating is 70 to 100 years old. Where it cracks, wires can short or spark.
  • Buried under attic insulation. K&T was built to cool off in open air. Blown-in insulation on top of it traps the heat.
  • Old do-it-yourself add-ons. Decades of splices and extensions tied into old circuits are often a bigger worry than the original wire.

How to Tell If a Calgary Home Has Knob-and-Tube

In Calgary you mostly find K&T in pre-1950 inner-city homes, in areas like Bridgeland, Inglewood, Ramsay, Sunnyside, Hillhurst, Mount Pleasant and Sunalta. It also hides in homes that look updated. A renovated main floor can have new drywall and new light fixtures while old circuits still run to the attic, the basement or an old addition.

Things to look for before you write an offer:

  • Single wires held by white ceramic knobs in an unfinished basement or attic
  • Two-prong outlets in the older parts of the house
  • An old fuse panel, or a small 60-amp service
  • A listing that says "updated electrical" but does not say the home was fully rewired

Then ask your home inspector one clear question: is any knob-and-tube still live? Insurers care most about active circuits. Old wire that has been cut off and left inside the walls is a very different conversation, as long as an electrician will confirm in writing that it is disconnected.

Is Knob-and-Tube Wiring Legal in Alberta?

Yes. Alberta does not require you to tear out knob-and-tube that is already in a house, and you can buy or sell a home that has it. Any new electrical work has to meet today's code, though, and in Calgary a rewire needs an electrical permit and inspections through the City.

That permit matters for your mortgage file. When an insurer asks for proof that the K&T is gone, the paperwork you want is the electrician's invoice showing what was replaced plus the permit with its final inspection passed. The City's homeowner electrical permit rules let owners who live in the home do some of their own wiring. Before closing you do not own the house yet, so any work done before possession is on the seller's side.

Why Insurance Companies Are So Careful With K&T

Insurers set prices from claims data, not from how one house looks. The Insurance Bureau of Canada recommends replacing knob-and-tube, and insurers treat it as a high-risk item. In practice you will usually get one of these answers:

  • A flat no while any K&T circuits are live
  • Coverage only after a licensed electrician inspects the system and writes a report
  • Coverage at a higher premium, often with a higher deductible
  • Coverage with a deadline to replace the wiring, sometimes as a condition of renewing the policy

The yes usually comes from an insurance broker who can shop several companies, including ones that write older homes. It costs more and takes longer to set up than a standard policy. Tell them about the wiring up front. Leaving it out of the application can put a future claim at risk.

Timing is what sinks these deals. A financing condition is often only a week or two. If the buyer calls one insurer, gets a no, and starts looking for another option with three days left, the file is in trouble. Start the insurance conversation the same day the inspection report lands.

What a Knob-and-Tube Rewire Costs in Calgary

As of 2026, Calgary electricians and cost guides commonly put a full knob-and-tube replacement at about $5,000 to $15,000 for a typical older home. Guides that price by size come in around $8 to $23 per square foot, so a bigger home can run well past $15,000. The price goes up when:

  • The walls and ceilings are finished plaster, so every opening needs patching
  • The home is larger or has two storeys
  • The panel needs an upgrade to 100 or 200 amps at the same time
  • There are old add-on circuits to trace and take out

Most rewires take a few days to a couple of weeks, plus drywall repair. Get two written quotes from licensed electricians. If you use a purchase plus improvements mortgage, you will need a written quote for the lender anyway.

What Your Lender Actually Checks

Your lender does not inspect the wiring. It looks at two things.

The appraisal. If the appraiser sees knob-and-tube, or the listing mentions it, it can end up in the report. A line like "knob-and-tube noted, no proof of replacement" can turn into a lender condition you have to clear before funding. Our guide to how a mortgage appraisal works in Canada explains what appraisers write down and why it matters.

Proof of insurance. Every lender needs the home insured, with the lender named on the policy, before it releases money on closing day. That is true at a big bank, a credit union, an alternative (B) lender and a private lender. A different type of lender can help with income, credit or a tight debt ratio. It does not get you around the insurance. If someone says a private lender "won't care" about the wiring, ask about the insurance, because that is where the file will stop.

Where the lender type does matter is how much of the repair it will finance. That is the third path below.

The Three Ways a Calgary K&T Deal Closes

Path 1: The seller replaces it before closing. The seller hires a licensed electrician, the permit is pulled and inspected, and the buyer gets the invoice and the permit paperwork. The buyer's insurance application is then a normal one. This is the cleanest path for insurance, but it needs time before possession. Make it a written term of the deal and have your realtor and lawyer word it clearly.

Path 2: A lower price, and the buyer rewires after possession. The seller drops the price by roughly the electrician's quote. The buyer arranges insurance that accepts K&T with a deadline to replace it, closes, and then does the rewire. One thing to know: a lower price means a smaller mortgage and a smaller down payment, but it does not put cash in your hand for the electrician. You still need that money on hand after closing.

Path 3: A purchase plus improvements mortgage. This is the option most buyers have never heard of. You get a written quote for the rewire. The lender approves you on what the home will be worth after the work is done and adds the cost of the work to the mortgage. At closing, the money for the work is held back. Your lawyer releases it once the job is done and checked, usually with invoices, photos or an inspection. Our full breakdown of how a purchase plus improvements mortgage works covers the steps.

The limits depend on the lender and on your down payment:

  • Insured mortgages (less than 20% down). At many banks and credit unions, the improvement amount is capped at around 10% of the after-improvement value, and the home has to be under $1.5 million. You can still go up to 95% loan-to-value on a one- or two-unit home.
  • 20% or more down. Some lenders allow a larger share of the value to go toward the work.
  • Alternative and some broker-only lenders. Some programs go to $100,000 or more, but the appraiser has to agree the work adds roughly dollar for dollar to the value.
  • Deadlines. Completion windows commonly run 90 to 120 days, and some lenders allow six months or more.

Two catches. Most electricians want a deposit, and the held-back money is only released after the work is done, so plan how you will cover that gap. And if you do the work yourself, most lenders count only the materials, not your time. For a first-time buyer who would otherwise need another $10,000 in cash on top of the down payment and closing costs, this path is often the difference between buying the house and walking away. If You Are Selling a Calgary Home With K&T

If your home still has live knob-and-tube, deal with it before the first buyer's inspector finds it. Two things go wrong for sellers: the deal falls apart at the financing condition, or the home gets priced lower than it needs to be because nobody knows what the fix costs.

  • Get a licensed electrician's inspection and a written rewire quote before you list.
  • Decide whether to replace it first, price the home to reflect it, or list with the quote in hand.
  • Give your realtor the report and the quote so buyer questions get answered right away.
  • Ask your realtor and lawyer what you need to disclose. Hiding a known problem can come back on you after closing.

In our experience, a seller who shows up with a quote and a plan protects the price better than a seller whose first deal falls apart in week two.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a mortgage on a Calgary home with knob-and-tube wiring?

Yes, in many cases, but insurance comes first. Every lender needs the home insured before it releases the money, and many insurers will not cover live knob-and-tube. The usual ways through are the seller replacing it before closing, insurance that accepts it with a deadline to replace it, or a purchase plus improvements mortgage that finances the rewire.

How much does it cost to replace knob-and-tube wiring in Calgary?

As of 2026, commonly about $5,000 to $15,000 for a typical older home. Guides that price by size put it at about $8 to $23 per square foot, so a larger home, plaster walls or a panel upgrade can push the cost higher. Get two written quotes from licensed electricians.

Will home insurance cover a house with knob-and-tube wiring?

Some insurers will not while the wiring is live. Others want an electrician's inspection report, charge a higher premium or deductible, or give you a deadline to replace it. An insurance broker who can shop several companies is usually the fastest way to find a yes. Tell them about the wiring up front.

Is knob-and-tube wiring legal in Alberta?

Yes. Alberta does not require existing knob-and-tube to be removed, and you can buy or sell a home that has it. New electrical work must meet the current code, and a rewire in Calgary needs an electrical permit and inspections.

Are there grants to replace knob-and-tube wiring in Alberta?

We could not find a provincial or federal grant for knob-and-tube replacement as of September 2026. For most buyers, the practical way to spread the cost is a purchase plus improvements mortgage, which adds the quoted cost of the rewire to the mortgage when you buy.

Can I add attic insulation over knob-and-tube wiring?

Not while the wiring is live. Knob-and-tube needs open air to get rid of heat, and burying it under insulation is one of the main reasons insurers worry about it. Have an electrician replace or disconnect those circuits first.

Can I put the cost of the rewire into my mortgage?

Yes, with a purchase plus improvements mortgage. You are approved on what the home will be worth after the work, and the money for the work is held back and released once the job is done and checked. On insured mortgages the improvement amount is often capped at around 10% of the after-improvement value, and some lenders allow larger amounts. Completion windows commonly run 90 to 120 days.

Should I buy a home with knob-and-tube wiring or walk away?

Run the numbers before your conditions come off. Get the electrician's quote, confirm you can insure the home, and see whether the price or a purchase plus improvements mortgage covers the rewire. If all three line up, the deal usually works. If you cannot get insurance in time, do not remove your financing condition.
