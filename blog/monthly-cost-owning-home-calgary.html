The True Cost of Owning a Home in Calgary, Month by Month

Book a Free Consultation

Pick a time that works for you — no obligation

A lot of people work out what a house costs by looking at one number: the mortgage payment. Then the first property tax bill shows up, the furnace needs a part in January, and the real number turns out to be a lot bigger.

This guide walks through the full cost of owning a home in Calgary, one line at a time. We'll use real 2026 figures where they exist, show you a sample monthly budget, and explain the gap between what a lender will approve and what you can live with.

The Six Costs Every Calgary Homeowner Pays

Every home comes with the same basic list. Some lines are fixed. Others swing with the seasons or with the age of the house.

  • The mortgage payment. Principal and interest. If you put less than 20% down, the default insurance premium is added to the loan, so it's built into this payment too.
  • Property tax. Billed by the City of Calgary once a year. Many people pay it monthly through the city's instalment plan or through their lender.
  • Home insurance. Your lender will ask for proof of coverage before your mortgage funds.
  • Utilities. Electricity, natural gas, water, sewer and waste. In Calgary, winter heating is the big swing.
  • Maintenance and repairs. The cost nobody bills you for until something breaks.
  • Condo fees, if you own a condo. These cover the building's shared costs and its reserve fund.

Before any of these start, there are one-time costs on closing day, like legal fees and the land title registration. Our guide to closing costs in Alberta covers those. This post is about what comes after you get the keys.

Property Tax: What a Typical Calgary Home Pays

Your property tax depends on your home's assessed value. The City sets that value every year and mails the assessment notice in January. Your tax bill has two parts: the city's share and the province's share, which the City collects for Alberta.

For 2026, the City says a typical single-family home is assessed at $706,000. Its municipal share is about $2,741 for the year. The provincial share went up by $338 this year, which puts the total bill for that home at roughly $4,700, or a little under $400 a month. A typical condo is assessed much lower, at a median of $347,000, so its bill is roughly half that.

Your home won't be exactly typical. The fastest way to check is the City's 2026 property tax breakdown and calculator. Plug in the assessed value of the home you're looking at before you make an offer. The current owner's tax bill is also fair to ask for.

Insurance, Utilities and Upkeep

Home insurance

Insurance has gone up a lot in Alberta after years of hail and flood claims. As of 2026, most estimates put the average Alberta homeowner policy somewhere around $1,500 to $2,300 a year, and some put Calgary higher because of hail. Plan for roughly $130 to $200 a month for a house, and get a real quote before you firm up an offer. Condo owners usually pay less, because the building's policy covers the structure.

Utilities

The City puts the typical metered house water bill at about $119 a month in 2026, and that includes water, sewer and drainage. Add electricity and natural gas, and most Calgary houses land somewhere around $300 to $600 a month all in. Summer is at the low end. A cold January in an older, drafty house is at the high end.

Ask the seller for a year of utility bills. It's a normal request, and it tells you more than any average can.

Maintenance and repairs

A common rule of thumb is to set aside 1% to 3% of the home's value every year for upkeep. On a $560,000 home, 1% is about $470 a month. Newer homes sit near the low end. An older house with an original roof, furnace or hot water tank should plan for more.

You won't spend this money every month. Some years it's a few hundred dollars for a plumber. Other years it's a $15,000 roof. Putting a set amount into a savings account each month keeps the big years from landing on a credit card.

A Sample Monthly Cost of Owning a Home in Calgary

Here's how the numbers add up for one example. Calgary's all-home benchmark price was about $564,000 in October 2026, so we'll use a $560,000 home bought with 10% down.

  • Mortgage payment: about $2,880. This uses a $504,000 mortgage plus the default insurance premium, a 25-year amortization and an example rate of 4.5%. That rate is only for illustration. Yours will depend on your file and the day you lock in.
  • Property tax: about $310. Based on an assessed value near $560,000.
  • Home insurance: about $130 to $200.
  • Utilities: about $300 to $600. Depending on the season and the house.
  • Maintenance savings: about $470. Using the 1% rule.

That's roughly $4,100 to $4,500 a month to own this home, and more in a cold month or an older house. The mortgage payment is only about two-thirds of it.

If you're coming from renting, compare this full number to your rent plus tenant insurance and utilities. That's the honest side-by-side. You're also building equity with part of every payment, which rent never does.

One more note on the mortgage line. With less than 20% down, the insurance premium generally runs from 2.8% to 4% of the loan, depending on your down payment, and it gets added to the mortgage. Our post on how mortgage default insurance works explains the math.

What Lenders Count, and What They Leave Out

This part surprises people. When a lender works out how much you can borrow, it doesn't use the full monthly cost. It uses a shorter list.

The first test is called your Gross Debt Service ratio, or GDS. It adds up your mortgage payment, property tax, a heating cost and half of any condo fee, then compares that total to your gross income. The second test, Total Debt Service or TDS, adds your other monthly debts like car loans and credit cards. Lenders also qualify you at a higher test rate than the one you'll actually pay.

The limits depend on the lender:

  • Banks and credit unions (A-lenders) generally cap GDS around 39% and TDS around 44% on insured mortgages.
  • Alternative lenders (B-lenders) can often stretch to roughly 50% on both, and they can look past things a bank won't, like a credit score under 600 depending on the rest of the file.
  • Private lenders lend mostly on equity. They usually want 20% to 25% or more down, and they cost more.

Notice what's missing from the lender's math: home insurance, electricity, water, maintenance and the other half of the condo fee. So you can be approved for a payment that leaves your monthly budget tight. The approval tells you the most a lender will lend. It doesn't tell you what's comfortable for your family.

That's why we build a real budget with clients before they shop, not after. A mortgage pre-approval in Calgary gives you the lender's number. Your budget gives you yours. Shop with the smaller one.

Condo or House: How the Monthly Cost Changes

Condos have a lower purchase price, a lower tax bill and usually a lower insurance bill. Calgary's apartment condo benchmark was about $289,000 in October 2026. With 10% down, the mortgage payment at the same example rate is around $1,480 a month, roughly half the house example.

The condo fee is what you have to watch. It can run from a couple of hundred dollars a month to well past that in an older building or one with a pool and gym. It usually covers building insurance, exterior upkeep, snow removal and the reserve fund, and sometimes heat and water. A low fee in an older building can mean an underfunded reserve fund and a special assessment later.

Before you buy a condo, read the reserve fund study, the latest financial statements and the board minutes. Look for planned repairs and any talk of a special assessment. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost per month to own a home in Calgary?

For a home near Calgary's 2026 benchmark price of about $560,000, bought with 10% down, the full monthly cost is roughly $4,100 to $4,500. That covers the mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities and money set aside for repairs. Your number depends on your down payment, rate, the home's age and its tax assessment.

How much is property tax on a house in Calgary?

For 2026, a typical Calgary single-family home assessed at $706,000 pays roughly $4,700 a year once the city and provincial shares are added together. A typical condo pays roughly half that. Use the City of Calgary's tax calculator with the home's assessed value to get a closer number.

How much should I budget for home maintenance?

A common rule of thumb is 1% to 3% of the home's value each year. On a $560,000 home, that's about $5,600 to $16,800 a year. Newer homes need less, and older homes with original roofs, furnaces or windows need more.

Do lenders include utilities and insurance when they qualify you?

Not fully. Lenders count the mortgage payment, property tax, a heating cost and half of any condo fee, plus your other debts. Home insurance, electricity, water and maintenance aren't part of the calculation, so it's worth building your own budget that includes them.

Is it cheaper to own a condo or a house in Calgary?

A condo usually costs less each month because the price, property tax and insurance are lower. The condo fee closes some of that gap, and an older building with a weak reserve fund can bring special assessments. Compare the full monthly cost of both, not just the price.
